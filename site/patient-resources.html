Patient Education

Patient Resources

Short, plain-language explanations of common spine topics. They are for general education only: they cannot diagnose your condition, and individual advice always requires a consultation.

What causes back pain?

Back pain usually arises from muscles, joints, discs, or ligaments in the spine. Common contributors include poor posture, unaccustomed activity, lifting, age-related change, and occasionally nerve involvement. Most back pain improves with time, movement, and simple care.

Sudden weakness in the legs, numbness in the saddle area, or loss of bladder/bowel control needs urgent medical evaluation.

What is a slipped disc?

A "slipped disc" (disc prolapse or herniation) means the soft centre of a spinal disc has pushed through a weak part of its outer wall. This can press on nearby nerves, causing back pain along with leg pain, tingling, or numbness. Many prolapses improve without surgery; treatment depends on symptoms and assessment.

When does sciatica need surgery?

Sciatica is leg pain caused by irritation of the sciatic nerve, often from a disc pressing on a nerve root. Most sciatica improves over weeks with conservative care. Surgery may be considered when pain is severe and persistent, when nerve function is affected, or when assessment shows a problem that surgery can address. The decision is always individual, based on clinical assessment and imaging.

What is endoscopic spine surgery?

Endoscopic spine surgery addresses certain spinal problems through very small incisions using a camera (endoscope) and fine instruments. It may allow less tissue disruption than open surgery for selected conditions. It is one option among several, and suitability depends on the diagnosis.

What is UBE?

Unilateral Biportal Endoscopy (UBE) is an endoscopic technique that uses two small portals - one for the camera, one for instruments - usually on one side of the spine. It gives the surgeon good visualisation and working room through tiny incisions, for selected spinal conditions.

When is spine surgery necessary?

Surgery is considered when: conservative care has not resolved a problem that surgery can address; symptoms significantly affect daily life; there is pressure on nerves with weakness or numbness; or, rarely, when a condition threatens nerve function. Necessity is decided per patient, from assessment and imaging - never by a website, including this one.

Recovery after spine surgery

Recovery varies with the procedure and the person. Guided recovery typically involves planned rest and early movement, graded return to activity, physiotherapy input where advised, pain management as prescribed, and scheduled follow-up visits to confirm progress and answer questions. Individual instructions come from your own surgical team.

Important

These articles are general education, not diagnosis. Symptoms such as leg weakness, saddle numbness, or bladder/bowel changes may need urgent evaluation. Nothing on this page should delay seeking care. All clinical content is subject to review by Dr. Abhinav Bhute before production publication.
